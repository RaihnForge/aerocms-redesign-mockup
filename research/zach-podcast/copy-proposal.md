# Mockup Copy Proposal — Aligning to Zach's Voice

**Source basis:** `zach-vision-analysis.md`
**Target file:** `c:\development\_queue\aerocms-redesign\index.html`
**Status:** Proposal — not yet applied. Awaiting Joshua's sign-off.

The structural work in the current mockup is solid (tier surfacing, partner stack, vCISO/SIEM/MDR/EDR named). These changes are tone and pitch alignment — moving from generic-consultative-MSP voice to Zach's actual "fully outsourced IT" pitch and his unapologetic price-confidence posture.

---

## Change 1 — Hero subline

**Before:**
> A managed services provider that learns your business before pitching a stack, helpdesk, leasing — operated as one practice, not four product lines.

**After:**
> Fully outsourced IT. Your monthly bill *is* your IT budget — we operate as your IT department, not as four product lines bolted together.

**Why:** Surfaces Zach's load-bearing pitch ("fully outsourced IT") on first paint. Keeps the "not four product lines" anti-stack-shopping line because it's already on-message.

---

## Change 2 — "Four pillars" section header

**Before:**
> **Four pillars. One partner.**
> Discover. Recommend. Implement. Manage. The IT stack you end up with is the one your business actually runs on.

**After:**
> **What it looks like to outsource your IT.**
> We don't sell tools. We become the IT department your business would have if it built one — staffed, mature, and accountable for the outcome.

**Why:** Replaces process-framing ("Discover. Recommend.") with outcome-framing. Drops the word "stack" — Zach actively avoids tech-product talk in his pitch.

---

## Change 3 — IT Business Solutions pillar

**Before:**
> Full-spectrum IT consulting and managed delivery. We understand the business, propose options, implement the strategy — and stay on as your team.

**After:**
> You don't manage a stack. We do. We learn how your business actually makes money, then build and run the IT department that supports it.

**Why:** Direct quote of Zach's framing ("how the business makes money → what tech supports it"). Confident first sentence echoes his blunt cadence.

---

## Change 4 — Security Solutions pillar

**Before:**
> Multi-layer protection sized to your risk. From basic patch hygiene to virtual CISO oversight — with a calculator to make sure you're not overspending.

**After:**
> Security at the level larger companies expect — vCISO oversight, 24/7 SIEM, MDR/EDR — without the in-house team to staff it. Quality costs money. We make sure you spend it on the right things.

**Why:** "Quality costs money" is a near-direct Zach quote. Positions security as enterprise-grade by default, not a tiered upsell.

---

## Change 5 — Cloud Services pillar

**Before:**
> Shared, dedicated, or hybrid — on Azure or VMware. Remote-first work, predictable spend, and an excessive-spending audit so you know you're sized right.

**After:**
> Public, private, or hybrid — sized to what your business actually does, not what a cloud vendor wants to sell you. Audit included so you stop overpaying.

**Why:** Inverts the vendor-led pitch. Same tone as Zach's "they don't care how the sausage is made" — keeps the focus on the business, not the platforms.

---

## Change 6 — Hardware as a Service pillar

**Before:**
> Lease, don't buy. Predictable monthly OpEx, a 3-year guarantee, and lifecycle handled end-to-end — from procurement to refresh.

**After:**
> Stop buying hardware. We lease and lifecycle the gear so it shows up on your books as predictable monthly OpEx — not a capital hit every three years.

**Why:** Tighter, more declarative. Same factual content; better cadence.

---

## Change 7 — Plans section intro

**Before:**
> **Plans built around how much IT you want to own.**
> Four tiers, plus the Kaseya User Bundle as an add-on across any of them. Convention over configuration on the lower tiers; full customization at Enterprise.

**After:**
> **Plans built around how much IT you want to own.**
> We don't sell hours. We sell outcomes priced to match how much of your IT we're running. Lower tiers give you the stack and the phone number; Enterprise we build to fit. Kaseya User Bundle adds on top of any tier.

**Why:** "We don't sell hours" maps to Zach's "all-inclusive, everything's in the bill" stance. Keeps the practical Kaseya add-on note.

---

## Change 8 — Closing CTA

**Before:**
> **Ready to make IT easy?**
> Tell us about your business. We'll come back with options sized to fit — not a brochure.

**After:**
> **Ready to outsource IT?**
> Tell us what your business does — not what tech you think you need. We'll come back with what working with us actually looks like, and what it costs. If we're not a fit, no hard feelings.

**Why:** Softer web version of Zach's "if expensive scares you away, it was a pleasure meeting you." The qualifying-by-value posture is the most distinctive thing about his sales motion — the closing CTA is where it earns its keep.

---

## Change 9 — NEW: Industry-vertical line under partner-logo strip

**Add this line above or below the "Working alongside the platforms your business already trusts" partner logo wall:**

> We know the businesses we serve — oilfield operations, infrared/thermal, robotics, professional services, light manufacturing — well enough to predict the problems before you describe them.

**Why:** Industry-vertical pattern recognition is one of Zach's core differentiators ("every business has the same prototypical set of struggles, categorized by vertical"). The BRIEF and XMind both name verticals the current site doesn't surface. Either inline near the logos or as its own short strip below.

---

## Summary

Net effect: the page **stops sounding like every other MSP redesign** and starts sounding like Zach. Same facts, same structure, same partner stack — but the voice carries his pitch instead of generic consultative-MSP boilerplate.

Lines that don't need to change (already on-message): the "Plans built around how much IT you want to own." heading, the pillar bullet lists, the resources block, the footer.
