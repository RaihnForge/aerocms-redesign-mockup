## Zach's Core Philosophy — His Own Words

### 1. "Fully outsourced IT" is the pitch

> "We have a particular philosophy that we've kind of developed and that is the concept of fully outsourced IT. We built a service offering that is all-inclusive."

> "When the client pays our monthly bill, their annual cost to us literally **is** their IT budget. We take the entire budget and just operate as if we are their IT department."

> "We are a fully staffed, fully mature IT department for you."

This is the load-bearing concept. Everything else hangs off it. The current mockup does not say this anywhere.

### 2. He leads with the price, on purpose

> "There isn't literally nothing we won't be open to discussing doing as part of your contract. And because of that, we are very expensive. If expensive scares you away, then it was a pleasure meeting you. If you change your mind, here's my card."

> "Sales cycle does the qualification very early — within the first 15 minutes."

His tone is unapologetic. "Very expensive" is a feature, not an embarrassment. He qualifies *by* value upfront so the rest of the conversation is with people who get it.

**Data point:** Most profitable client is a 6-employee law firm paying **$469/seat**. (Industry comparable: $30–$50/seat per MSP forums.)

### 3. He sells business transformation, not technology

> "The overarching theme is always: this is how technology transforms business. We don't talk about the technology and how cool it is, and how whiz-bang it is, with all the widgets and the gizmos."

> "They don't care how the sausage is made. They just want to know why my sausage is so much better than everybody else's sausage."

Active avoidance: brand-name stack lists, feature lists, technical jargon. Active emphasis: revenue impact, efficiency, scale, business maturity.

### 4. He learned business first, technology second

> "I was always very technical. I was an engineer. What I had to learn was what does that mean to a business? What activities does the staff perform that lead to revenue? Then look at how they do it and ask: what technology can further that goal?"

This is the consultative posture in the BRIEF. It comes from his own arc.

### 5. Brand-awareness through education, never pitch

> "Aggressive brand awareness. Connect with every business association, chamber of commerce — anything where business owners and stakeholders gather. Host a learning event. **It's never a sales pitch.** I spend 30 minutes explaining how Office 365 transforms businesses, and this is why."

Implication for the site: the homepage CTA path and the resources section should *behave* like Zach in the room — give value first, ask for nothing, qualify by interest.

### 6. Industry-vertical pattern recognition

> "Every business has the same prototypical set of struggles. Usually categorized by industry vertical — accountancy firms have the same struggles across all accountancy firms. Service-based industries — industrial controls, manufacturing, HVAC — do the same thing and struggle with the same thing."

Per the BRIEF, the XMind names verticals: oilfield, infrared/thermal, robotics. Currently invisible on the site.

### 7. Size is a frame of mind

> "I don't think you need to be a big company to handle bigger deals. How do you define a big company? I have 30 people at Benchmark on my team — does that not count? If you don't see yourself as a small company, then you shouldn't be afraid of complex deals."

> "Pipeline has six deals in excess of 1,000 seats. Smallest client: 5 seats. Largest: 7,000."

His confidence isn't bluster — it's earned by treating Benchmark 365 as his delivery team, not a vendor.

### 8. Large clients vs. small clients — invert the assumption

> "Larger companies understand that quality costs big money and they have the money to spend. They don't see it as 'Karen wasn't happy' — they see it as a whole."

> "Smaller companies want concierge level white glove service — deliver it for them, but charge accordingly. That's why my smaller clients are on paper more profitable than my large clients."

Counter-intuitive truth for the site: *small* clients pay premium for personal attention; *large* clients pay premium for scale and reliability. Both tiers are profitable when priced honestly.

## Mockup Gap Analysis

The current homepage is structurally sound (tiered plans surfaced, partner stack named, vCISO/SIEM/MDR/EDR called out per the XMind). What's missing is **Zach's pitch and his voice**.

| # | Current copy | Gap | Zach's frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero subline: "A managed services provider that learns your business before pitching a stack…" | Generic consultative MSP talk. Could be any firm. | "Fully outsourced IT. Your monthly bill *is* your IT budget." |
| 2 | "Four pillars. One partner." | Service-list framing. | Service pillars are fine, but the wrapper should be transformation-framed, not service-framed. |
| 3 | "Discover. Recommend. Implement. Manage. The IT stack you end up with is the one your business actually runs on." | Process-framed. Close to right but still talks about the stack, not the business. | Stack is invisible to the client — they care about the business outcome it delivers. |
| 4 | IT Business Solutions: "Full-spectrum IT consulting and managed delivery." | Industry jargon. | "We become your IT department." |
| 5 | Security Solutions: "Multi-layer protection sized to your risk." | Defensive framing. | Quality-costs-money framing. |
| 6 | "Plans built around how much IT you want to own." | Good — already Zach-shaped. | Keep. |
| 7 | "Ready to make IT easy? Tell us about your business. We'll come back with options sized to fit — not a brochure." | Soft CTA. | Could lead with the qualifying stance: this is what working with us looks like, and what it costs. |
| 8 | No "fully outsourced IT" language anywhere on page | The #1 thing Zach says about his company doesn't appear. | Surface in hero or as a section heading. |
| 9 | No industry-vertical proof (oilfield, infrared/thermal, robotics, accountancy, manufacturing, HVAC per Zach & BRIEF) | Trust signal missing. | "We've learned what makes [verticals] tick." |
| 10 | Resources section is generic-MSP-helpful | Zach gives free value as a brand strategy. | Lean harder into "education, not pitch" — these are the talks Zach gives at chambers. |

---

## Recommended Copy Direction (one-line summary)

> **Stop describing services. Start describing what it looks like to outsource your IT department.**

The hero, the four pillars wrapper, the plans intro, and the closing CTA all need to shift from *what we sell* to *what you get when you stop running IT yourself.* The price-confidence and business-transformation pitch should bleed through every section.
